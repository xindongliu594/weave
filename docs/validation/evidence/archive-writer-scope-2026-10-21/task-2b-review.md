### Important findings

1. **Nested withWriter bypasses invocation expiry and failure latching.** `services/agent-runtime/archive-writer-scope.mjs:95` directly calls `fn(borrowed)` in the parent's ALS context, unlike the tracked `owner.run` path at `:99` and producer tracking at `:46`–`:56`. Consequently, an AsyncResource callback captured in an already-completed nested invocation remains live while the outer invocation is live, despite no prior retain. More materially, when the outer caller catches a nested callback rejection, no latch records that known failure: the outer helper subsequently resolves writerDrained and releases the original guard. This violates the required failure-preserving lifetime contract and the claimed individual-invocation binding. Execute nested callbacks through the same tracked invocation mechanism (without acquiring another native guard or closing parent admission), expire their own context on settlement, and latch their failures even when an outer caller catches the rejection. Add regressions for both cases, while preserving already-retained producer tails.

2. **A borrowed view can retain a different invocation's original owner.** `services/agent-runtime/archive-writer-scope.mjs:94` validates whichever context is currently in ALS through `assertContext(id)`, but then retains the *captured* `context.state.owner`. The validation at `:29` checks manager/run/live status, not equality with the captured invocation/state. Save a borrowed view from owner A, end A's run invocation without closing its admission, acquire independent owner B for the same run, and call the saved view's retain inside B.run: it succeeds and creates a producer on A. Thus an unrelated call chain can borrow A's lifetime instead of being restricted to its own independently acquired owner. Bind the retain-only view to its exact originating live invocation and validate that identity before retaining. Explicitly retained producer handles should continue to be transferable; the unretained borrowed view should not. Add a cross-owner same-run negative test and a stale-view negative test.
